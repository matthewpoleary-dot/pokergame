# The pattern worth copying — verticalised operations software

Date: 2026-09-20. Prompted by tighsauna.com. Method: web search; most vendor sites were
blocked by network egress policy, so this rests on search summaries.

## The play

Take a physical, session-based, capacity-constrained business category that is booming. Build
the thing that runs it: bookings, payments, memberships, waitlists, retention. Sell it to the
operators at €50-150/month. Win by being built *for that category* rather than being a
generic scheduler they have to bend into shape.

Worked examples:

- **tigh** — saunas. "Bookings, payments and reasons to come back. Built around your sauna."
  **Roughly one month old as of September 2026.** A polished site and a pricing page, not yet
  evidence of a working market — see the caution below.
- **Golf O'Clock** — indoor golf simulators. 200+ active facilities, 1.2m bookings processed.
  This is the one with actual proof attached.
- **Playtomic** — padel and racket sports. Dominant in Europe, €56m raised.

The buyers are ideal: they have revenue, they are unsophisticated software purchasers, they
are currently on Squarespace plus Instagram DMs or a generic booking tool, and they measure
the product against lost revenue rather than against €5/month.

## Why it beats everything else screened in this project

Seven consumer software ideas died because anything an outsider finds by searching has already
been found by everyone else running that search. This play is different on two counts: the
buyer is a business with money, and the categories keep being *created* — saunas, cold plunge,
simulator golf, padel and pickleball barely existed as venue categories five years ago. New
categories mean new windows.

## The screen

1. **Is the category booming and venue-based?** New venues opening, session-based, capacity to
   fill.
2. **Is there a dominant player?** The only hard kill. Playtomic in European padel is a kill.
   Golf O'Clock at 200 of tens of thousands of venues is not.
3. **What are operators using today?** If the answer is Squarespace, Fresha, Calendly or a
   spreadsheet, the window is open.
4. **Is the leader region-locked?** This is the most reliable seam — see below.
5. **Can you reach 50 operators without ad spend?** They congregate: trade groups, suppliers,
   Instagram, category subreddits, conferences.

50 venues × €100/month is €60,000 a year. The bar was €500-2,000/month; this clears it.

## The seam that is actually open: geography

The incumbents are region-locked in both directions. Playtomic owns European padel while the
US and Middle East are explicitly described as underserved; CourtReserve and PlayByPoint are
priced in dollars for American clubs. The same asymmetry recurs across these categories,
because payment rails, VAT handling, language and support hours are genuinely different and a
US-first product does them badly.

"Same vertical, different region" is therefore a live play that needs no new category — and it
is what an Irish founder is well placed to do for Europe.

## The uncomfortable part

I cannot hand over the next category, and it is worth being precise about why. By the time a
category is visible enough for me to find it by search, it is visible to everyone else running
the same search — the same structural fact that killed the seven consumer ideas, one level up.

Tigh's founder did not find saunas by searching. They were in that scene while it was
happening — and they are a friend of this project's author, which makes the point sharper
rather than weaker. The input that produced the only genuinely promising idea in this entire
exploration was proximity to someone doing the thing, not research.

Which means the next one is inside a scene already being lived in, watching operators do
something badly with tools that do not fit. That is an observation task, not a research task,
and it is the one input that cannot be outsourced.

## Caution on treating tigh as validation

An earlier draft of this document leaned on tigh as evidence that this play works. At roughly
one month old that is not established. A pricing page and a customers page show that a founder
believes in the market, which is not the same as operators paying. Before copying the pattern,
establish how many venues are actually paying and what they were using before. Golf O'Clock's
200 facilities are evidence; a month-old landing page is a hypothesis.

**What can be outsourced:** naming a category takes one search to kill-test against the screen
above. Ten categories have been tested this way in about an hour.
