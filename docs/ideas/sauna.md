# Idea assessment — outdoor sauna operation (the Tigh'n Alluis model)

Date: 2026-09-20. Method: web search. **Confidence caveat:** tighsauna.com / tighnalluis.ie
and most operator sites were blocked by this session's network egress policy, so figures come
from search summaries, press coverage and council pages. Verify prices and licence terms
directly before acting.

## Why this is structurally better than every software idea we screened

Seven software ideas died in one session, all the same way: anything an outsider can find by
searching has already been found by the thousands of people running that same search.
**Outsider-visible niches are picked clean by construction.**

A physical, sited business inverts that. Nobody in Ohio can clone a sauna in Glencullen. The
moat is geography and physical effort, which do not scale and therefore do not attract
clones. That is the whole reason this is available and the software ideas were not.

Second, willingness to pay is proven and an order of magnitude above anything in the software
plan. We spent an hour arguing about whether a poker host would pay €5/month. These operators
charge **€22-25 per person for 60 minutes**, and sell out.

## The model, as Tigh'n Alluis runs it

- Location: Glencullen, Dublin Mountains — **private land, not a public beach.**
- Barrel sauna, up to 8 people: **€22 per person / 60 min.**
- Yurt sauna, up to 14 people: **€25 per person / 60 min.**
- Cold plunge barrels and a natural pond for the contrast.
- Booked online, by the session.
- Also trades at festivals (All Together Now), which is incremental revenue off the same kit.

At capacity that is €176/session for the barrel and **€350/session for the yurt**.

## Unit economics, conservatively

Assume one 8-10 person unit, weekends only, 65% average fill, 4 sessions a Saturday and
Sunday:

| | Figure |
|---|---|
| Revenue per session (8 seats × €22 × 65%) | ~€115 |
| Per weekend (8 sessions) | ~€920 |
| Per year (45 weekends) | **~€41,000 gross** |
| Direct costs (hardwood, insurance, cleaning, card fees, site share) | ~40-50% |
| **Net, one unit, weekends only** | **~€20,000-25,000** |

A 14-person yurt roughly doubles the top line for the same operating effort, which is why
Tigh'n Alluis runs one. Add weekday evenings, private hire and festivals and a single
well-run site plausibly reaches €40-60k gross.

That clears the "too low" bar by 2-3x compared with the micro-SaaS path, for comparable
weekly hours — and the hours are weekend-shaped, which actually fits "20 hours in bursts"
better than SaaS does.

## Capital required

- **DIY barrel build: €2,500-5,000** depending on size and materials.
- Pre-built barrel: from €6,000.
- Mid-range, well-insulated, year-round Irish conditions: €9,000-15,000.
- 10-person mobile barrel on a trailer: **€18,950.**
- Plus stove, cold plunge, changing provision, and **public liability insurance requiring an
  indemnity limit of not less than €6.5m.**

Realistic entry: **€5,000-15,000.** This is the actual barrier, and it is precisely why the
opportunity still exists. Capital is the filter that keeps out everyone doing what we did all
afternoon.

## The licence situation, and the important loophole

On public land, permission is rationed and demand for it hugely exceeds supply:

- **Dún Laoghaire-Rathdown** ran the first Dublin pilot. Up to **15 applications**, from which
  only **5 saunas across 3 locations** were licensed, permitted 7am-7pm, **three days a week**.
  Its floating-sauna tender in January 2026 drew **30 applications**.
- **Fingal** is running a pilot: **€600 + VAT for six months.**
- **Sligo**: €225 for July-September, €360 for the full July-December period.
- **Cork**: up to €1,400 per location, with West Cork operators reported facing close to
  €10,000 a year across multiple sites — a live controversy about pricing operators out.
- **Galway**: no levy for trading at beaches.

Read that as two facts. Demand is proven — 30 applicants for one pitch. And a beach licence
is a lottery you can lose.

**The loophole is what Tigh'n Alluis does: operate on private land.** No casual trading
licence, no council rationing, no three-days-a-week cap, no competing against 30 applicants.
You need a site instead — which is a relationship, not an auction.

Note one distinction to check: a unit on a genuine trailer chassis is generally treated as a
temporary structure and in many standard cases needs no planning permission, whereas a fixed
installation on private land may. Keep it trailer-mounted and mobile until you know.

## The honest problem

**It cannot be run from abroad.** Lighting stoves, hauling dry hardwood, cleaning between
sessions, being on site. A sauna business with no operator present is a closed sauna. You
leave for Erasmus in January, and that is a harder conflict than anything in the software
plans, because software keeps selling while you are away and this does not.

Three ways through, in order of preference:

1. **Line it up now, launch in June.** The Erasmus term is phone-and-email work: find the
   site, agree terms, get insurance quotes, confirm the council position, order or build the
   unit. Open when you are back, into the summer, with autumn and winter — which is peak
   sauna-and-cold-plunge season — ahead of you.
2. **Partner on land, or on capital, or both.** A landowner with water access takes a revenue
   share and puts in no cash; you bring the unit, the operations and the marketing. This also
   solves the capital problem if the partner funds the unit. Farmers, GAA clubs, golf clubs,
   caravan parks, glamping sites and hotels all have the asset and none of the interest in
   running it.
3. **Mobile events only** — festivals, stags, corporate days, sports clubs. Highest revenue
   per day, no site needed, but seasonal, and a trailer is required from the start.

## What to verify before spending anything

1. **A site.** Private land within ~45 minutes of Dublin with water — pond, lake, river or
   sea access. This is the whole business; everything else is procurable. Start here.
2. **An insurance quote** at €6.5m public liability, as a real number from a broker who has
   written this class before. If this is prohibitive, stop.
3. **Planning status** of a trailer-mounted unit on the specific site, in writing from the
   council, plus whether your intended use counts as commercial activity requiring anything
   else.
4. **Water, changing and toilet provision** requirements.
5. **Local saturation** — the Dublin coast already has The Hot Box, The Sea Sauna at Portrane
   and the Dún Laoghaire floating sauna. The mountains and inland water are less contested,
   which is exactly where Tigh'n Alluis sits.

## Correction: the software play was the real opportunity, not the operation

The original version of this document ended by advising against building software for sauna
operators — "the micro-SaaS trap, a market of a few hundred businesses in Ireland." That was
wrong, and the error is instructive.

**tighsauna.com** is exactly that product: "Fill the house. Bookings, payments and reasons to
come back. Built around your sauna." Pricing page, demo booking, a customers page. An Irish
name, built into the Irish sauna boom, sold globally.

The mistake was scoping the market to Ireland. The market is the worldwide sauna and
cold-plunge boom — thousands of venues, each able to pay €50-150/month for the system that
runs their bookings. That is a materially larger business than operating one sauna, with no
capital requirement, no council licence, no hardwood to haul, and no need to be physically
present on a beach — which also resolves the Erasmus conflict that makes the operating plan
awkward.

So this file's conclusion inverts: **operating a sauna is a decent €20-25k/yr weekend
business; building the operating system for sauna venues is the actual opportunity, and
someone is already executing it.**
